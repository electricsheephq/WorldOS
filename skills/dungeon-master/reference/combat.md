# Combat playbook — running a fight through the engine

The story rules still apply in a fight (NPCs/foes SPEAK, the danger is felt) — but the mechanics are
unforgiving, so source every one from the engine:

- **EVERY fight runs through the engine — NO prose-only combat, ever, not even a trivial or one-sided one.** The instant a blow is struck, `start_combat`; resolve each side's actions with `attack` / `cast_spell` / `saving_throw` so the dice actually roll and the engine tracks HP; close with `end_combat` (which awards the XP). Narrating "they take the enforcer in seconds, Wyll drops him with a blast" — with no rolls, no initiative, no damage — is the ONE thing that breaks the plugin's load-bearing promise (deterministic mechanics) at the exact moment it matters, and the behavioral gate + scorer treat it as critical. If the CR gap is trivial, `spawn_monster` the foe with LOW hp so the fight is *short* — but the dice MUST roll. A drawn sword is a `start_combat`, never a sentence.

- **Honor the campaign's house rules.** Read `get_house_rules(campaign_id)` and adjudicate to it: lean the encounter to `difficulty` (easy/standard/hard), apply `critical_max_damage` when set (crits add the max die value instead of doubling dice), and only nudge a roll if `dm_can_fudge` is true (off by default — otherwise the dice stand). It also returns `flanking_advantage`, `slow_natural_healing`, `feats_allowed`, and `multiclass_allowed`.

- **Put monsters on the field with `spawn_monster(name)`** — it builds a full, combat-ready stat block (HP, AC, abilities, resistances/immunities, attacks) from the SRD bestiary, so you never hand-transcribe stats or guess. Use `count` for a pack (`spawn_monster("Goblin Warrior", count=3)`). Named adventure villains and any NPC with a stat block (e.g. Grett, Quill) are **already** combat-ready — fight their *existing* record; never create a second one for the same character.
- Pass a damage type to `attack`/`apply_damage` (e.g. `damage_type="fire"`) so the engine applies the target's resistance/immunity/vulnerability automatically.
- `start_combat` rolls initiative and sets the turn to the **first** combatant — that combatant acts *immediately*. Do **not** call `next_turn` before the first turn.
- After a combatant finishes its action, call `next_turn` once to advance. The engine skips dead/removed combatants for you — never double-advance to "skip" someone.
- Track the **action economy** with `use_action(kind=action|bonus|reaction)`: each creature gets one action + one bonus action on its turn and one reaction per round (it returns `ok:false` if something tries to act twice or off-turn). Multiattack / Extra Attack is **one** action — declare a single `action`, then make several `attack` calls under it.
- **SUBCLASS resource pools aren't seeded by the SRD tables — register them yourself so the first fight can track them.** The engine auto-derives only *base-class* pools (Rage, Ki, Second Wind, Action Surge, Sorcery Points, Channel Divinity, …). A subclass / feat / homebrew resource — a Battle Master's **Superiority Dice**, a Psi Warrior's **Energy Dice**, an Arcane Archer's **Arcane Shots** — is invisible until you `set_class_resource`. **Supply the CORRECT level-scaled count from the rules** — the engine ships no non-SRD numbers, so a wrong value goes in unchecked. Battle Master Superiority Dice: **4** at levels 3–6, **5** at 7–14, **6** at 15+, die d8 (→d10 at 10, →d12 at 18) — so a level-5 Battle Master is `set_class_resource(campaign_id, character_id, "superiority_dice", 4, "short", "d8")`. Do this at character creation for any subclassed martial/caster; then spend it with `use_resource` and let rests recharge it.
- **A player MANEUVER with a rider is the same TWO steps as a monster's — don't drop the secondary effect.** A Battle Master's Trip Attack (target STR save or knocked **prone**), Pushing Attack (STR save or pushed), Menacing (WIS save or **frightened**), a Battle Master's Disarming, a Rune Knight's etc. — `use_resource` spends the die and you `attack` the primary hit, then resolve the rider: `saving_throw` the target vs the maneuver DC (8 + the attacker's proficiency + the relevant ability mod) and `add_condition`/narrate on a fail. Run the save **even when the hit kills** — skipping "it died anyway" trains the wrong habit and desyncs the next, non-lethal use.
- Each `attack` / `cast_spell` / `saving_throw` is for the **current** turn-holder (see `get_state.current_turn`). Acting for someone else mid-combat is a reaction; the engine returns an `off_turn_warning` — heed it so the initiative order doesn't desync.
- `attack` **already applies its own damage** on a hit (and reports the target's new state) — do **not** call `apply_damage` again afterward, or you'll hit twice. Use `apply_damage` only for damage that isn't an attack (a failed save, a trap, environmental).
- **SOURCE the to-hit + damage modifier from the SHEET — never invent or copy one.** `attack(attack_bonus=…, damage_dice='1d8+3')` TRUSTS the number you pass; a wrong bonus rolls a wrong die and silently breaks determinism. Each character's `get_character` (and `start_character`'s return) carries a **`combat_numbers`** block — `melee_attack_bonus` (STR weapon), `ranged_attack_bonus` (DEX/finesse), the damage mods, proficiency, every ability mod. Read it for the acting combatant before its first `attack`; never reuse another combatant's bonus (QA: a Rogue's to-hit was narrated +7 by copying the party's vampire when her sheet gave +3). A monster's to-hit/damage is in its `spawn_monster` stat-block notes — use those, not a guess.
- **A monster attack with a SECONDARY rider is TWO steps — don't drop the rider.** Many stat blocks deal primary damage PLUS an extra effect on a save (an imp's sting: piercing *and* a Con save vs 2d6 poison; a ghoul's claws *and* a paralysis save). `attack` only rolls the primary you pass — so resolve the rider yourself: `attack` for the primary hit, then `saving_throw` the target vs the rider's DC and `apply_damage(half=<saved>)` (or `add_condition` on a fail). The bestiary lists the rider in the creature's action text; read it. And `start_combat` echoes who has **Extra Attack** (`attacks_per_action`) — make that many `attack` calls under the one action; it's the easiest thing to forget.
- **Positional adv/dis is YOURS to pass in theater-of-mind.** The engine auto-detects *condition*-based advantage/disadvantage (a prone/unconscious target, etc. — set `is_ranged=True` so a prone target correctly gives a *ranged* attack disadvantage, not advantage), but it can't see positions you only narrated. SRD: **a ranged attack (or ranged spell attack) made while a hostile is within 5 ft of the ATTACKER is at disadvantage** — when you've put an enemy in your shooter's/caster's face, pass `disadvantage=True`. (Or run the fight with zones, below, and the geometry is explicit.) Don't let an archer fire point-blank into a melee with no penalty — it's a real swing in the math and, unchecked, it can decide a death.
- For a **save spell**, get the DC from `spell_save_dc` (never compute it by hand — items/proficiency vary), then `saving_throw` the target, then `apply_damage(half=<the save succeeded>)`.
- On EVERY companion turn call `companion_suggest_action` fresh and play it in the companion's voice (deviate only with reason). If it returns `aid_downed`/`heal`, cast the suggested `spell` that turn (`cast_spell` → `apply_healing`) — never let an ally bleed out across rounds with a heal in hand. If `aid_downed` comes back with `spell: null` (no slot left), `stabilize(actor_id, target_id)` the downed ally (a DC 10 Medicine check) instead of hand-waving it.
- **End the fight with `end_combat`** when the field is clear. In the default "xp" leveling mode it **auto-awards the defeated monsters' XP** to the party (split) and returns per-character `grants` with `can_level_up` — when someone *can* level, OFFER it in the fiction ("the fight's edge has changed you — you can train up") and run `level_up` when they choose. Don't silently pocket a level-up; let the player feel they earned it.
- **Commit the killing blow through the engine BEFORE you narrate a death.** A foe you describe as dropping or dying must first be brought to 0 HP via `attack`/`apply_damage` (which sets `dead`) — THEN narrate it. A narrated death the engine never recorded loses the kill: no XP, and the 'corpse' still stands in the state.
- **`end_combat` only after the last foe is actually down in the engine.** Ending combat while an enemy is still at 1 HP, then narrating the finish, runs the kill outside the encounter — the engine will still award XP at that later `apply_damage`, but the illusion of a clean fight sequence is broken and the gate will catch any XP that goes missing.
- **Never reference your own token / time / cost budget in the fiction.** If you must pause, end on a clean in-world scene break — never an in-character 'I'm out of budget' line.
- **A WANDERING ENCOUNTER is TYPED — run it PER ITS `type`, never narrate it away.** Overland travel (`travel_to` with `advance_time=True`), a camp's overnight (`long_rest`), and the explicit `roll_wandering_encounter` can return a **`wandering_encounter`** key. It now ALWAYS carries a **`type`** — and **most are NOT fights**. Travel and camp should feel VARIED: a road-meeting you talk past, a washed-out ford you skill-check, a hazard you save against, a quiet boon — every one of those IS a wandering encounter, not just an ambush. Branch on `type`:
  - **`combat`** → `{type:"combat", region, difficulty, surprise, foes:[{name, count, cr, xp_each, ids}], encounter_xp, outlook:{band, overmatch_ratio, must_offer_out, guidance}}`. The foes are **already spawned** (sized to your party), so this is the same load-bearing rule as every fight: the ambush erupts → `start_combat(campaign_id, [...party ids..., ...foe ids...])` (the `ids` are in the payload) → resolve through the engine → `end_combat`. Do **not** describe the patrol melting into the trees when the engine staged a fight — that's the prose-only-combat failure the gate treats as critical, just sprung on the road. **Honor `surprise`:** `true` → narrate the ambush landing first + run a surprise round; `false` → a clean clash, everyone rolls. **The `outlook` is the balancing doctrine, folded in:** read it like a built-in `encounter_outlook` — when **`outlook.must_offer_out` is true**, you MUST surface a cost-bearing OUT (escape leaving something behind, parley/relent, a hazard that buys retreat) via `generate_parley_options` before the dice fall, per the balancing doctrine below. No need to call `encounter_outlook` yourself for a staged wandering fight — it's right there in the payload.
  - **`skill`** → `{type:"skill", challenge, skill, dc}`. A region obstacle (a washed-out ford, a rockfall, a locked relay-gate). Narrate it, then resolve with **`skill_check(actor, skill, dc)`** — the suggested `skill` and `dc` are sheet-honest; don't hand-compute.
  - **`social`** → `{type:"social", who, stance, skill, dc}`. A road-meeting (a wary patrol, a desperate pilgrim, a hostile-but-talkable toll-bravo). **Voice the NPC + a Parley moment** (the `stance` sets the tone), then route the pick — a chosen line vs this scene extra → **`social_check`** vs the `dc`. This is a parley on the road, not a fight.
  - **`hazard`** → `{type:"hazard", peril, save_or_skill, dc}`. An environmental danger. Narrate it, then run a **`saving_throw`** (if `save_or_skill` is an ability — str/dex/con/…) or a **`skill_check`** (if it's a skill) vs the `dc`; `apply_damage`/narrate the consequence on a fail.
  - **`boon`** → `{type:"boon", find}`. A small positive (a cache, a friendly hunter, a shortcut). **Just narrate it** — no roll, no resolution. A breather is a legitimate beat.

  (All disabled per-campaign by `house_rules.wandering_encounters = False`; combat is never auto-started, so the `start_combat` is always yours to call.)

## Balancing doctrine — always a way out at low level; real stakes at high (HARD rule)

The world is **SET**, not level-scaled: the dragon's den has a dragon, and the engine **never** alters a single hit point to pull a punch. But a SET world will routinely put a low-level party in front of something that would simply wipe them — and a casual TPK of a level-3 party against a monster they had no chance against is a failure, not drama. The engine makes the over-match **objective** so this isn't a judgment call you can rationalize past:

- **Before staging or springing any fight that might be lopsided, call `encounter_outlook(campaign_id, monster_ids=[...])`** (or pass `monster_xps`). It returns `{band, overmatch_ratio, avg_party_level, must_offer_out, guidance}`. `overmatch_ratio` is the adjusted encounter XP over the party's *deadly* budget — the signal the `band` alone can't give you (the band caps at "deadly" and can't tell a winnable 1.12× troll from a 6.25× dragon). This is the same wire-it-through-the-engine discipline as `combat_numbers` for to-hit: read the authoritative number, don't eyeball the threat. **A `type:"combat"` wandering encounter already carries this `outlook` in its payload** — for a staged road/camp fight you don't re-call the tool, you read `wandering_encounter.outlook` and obey the same `must_offer_out` rule.
- **`must_offer_out == true` (ratio ≥ 2.0 AND average party level ≤ 5) is a HARD requirement, not a suggestion.** The fight is in the unwinnable zone. Do **NOT** auto-soften it (the dragon stays a dragon — clamping its CR is the one thing the owner forbids) and do **NOT** TPK them. **REQUIRED: frame the encounter as an Event WITH AN OUT, and the out must carry a COST** — escape leaving something behind, a parley/relent that becomes a deal, a hazard or collapse that buys a retreat, reinforcements that arrive at a price. Surface that branch as real player options via `generate_parley_options` (see story-craft), then let the dice fall on whatever they choose. "Always a way out" is **not** "always an easy way out": the out has teeth, the fight stays lethal if they pick it anyway.
- **At average party level ≥ 6, the stakes are real and a chosen fight can kill.** No `must_offer_out` fires; a deadly-but-winnable fight (`band == "deadly"`, ratio < 2.0, or any fight above level 5) is meant to be sweated, not escaped. Don't manufacture an exit they didn't earn — at that point a death they walked into is the game working. The flag exists to keep the *low-level* game survivable-but-tense, never to make every fight escapable.

## Tactical zones (optional — use when terrain matters)

By default combat is theater-of-the-mind and position is just narration. When the **terrain** carries the scene — a doorway to hold, rafters to climb to, an altar dais to reach, a chasm between the party and the archers — give the fight a positional spine with **named zones** (not a grid):

- **`set_zones(campaign_id, zones)`** once, at the top of the fight. Each zone is `{name, description?, adjacent?}`; `adjacent` lists the zones one move away (wire each edge once — it's symmetric). Skip it entirely for an open brawl. Then **`place_combatant(id, zone)`** each fighter into their starting region.
- **Range is advisory, never a wall.** A **melee** `attack` (and a touch spell via `cast_spell(target_id=…, is_melee=True)`) wants attacker and target in the **same or an adjacent** zone — out of reach surfaces a `range_warning`, but the attack still resolves (the engine never hard-blocks). **Ranged** attacks/spells reach **any** zone. With no zones declared, nothing is gated.
- **Movement: `move_to_zone(id, zone)`.** Leaving a zone that still holds a **hostile** returns `opportunity_attack: true` with the `provokers` — resolve each one's reaction yourself (an `attack`, tracked with `use_action(kind=reaction)`); the engine won't auto-roll it. A non-adjacent hop is flagged (maybe a Dash) but allowed.
- **AoE: `combatants_in_zone(zone)`** lists everyone standing there — your target list for a fireball or a dragon's breath, to `saving_throw` + `apply_damage` in turn.

## Running an ENSEMBLE — companions are their own agents (multi-agent QA, S3)

In an ensemble run (`qa/run_party.sh`) the party isn't yours to puppet: the player AND each companion is a **separate agent** acting through the **same constrained move facade** you resolve for the player. Each beat reaches you as a block of **structured moves tagged by actor** — `PLAYER (Kield): [do] …  [say] …`, then `Seraphine (companion): [cast] Cure Wounds  [say] "…"`, then the next companion, in roster order. Treat each one exactly like a player declaration:

- **Resolve, don't invent.** A companion's moves are its OWN choices — roll/cast/attack/use through the engine and narrate the **result**, voicing the companion as you do. NEVER decide a companion's *internal* choice for it (who it targets, whether it helps, whether it turns): that came from its own agent. You own the world's reaction and the dice; you do not own a companion's will. (This is the inverse of a solo run, where `companion_suggest_action` drives the ally — in an ensemble the ally drives itself.)
- **A betrayal is REAL combat, never narration.** An adversarial companion can carry a *sealed* agenda you are NOT told (it lives only in that agent's prompt, never in campaign state — you will never see it, and you should never try to guess or pre-empt it). When it turns, it arrives as a legal move — `Grok (companion): [attack] Kield with Greataxe` — so resolve it as an attack: roll initiative if needed, roll to hit, apply damage, and let the table feel the knife go in. Do not soften a companion's `[attack]` on an ally into a near-miss of narration or a change of heart; the engine makes the treachery real, and your job is to dramatize the fallout.
- **Everyone present SPEAKS, and the turn lands on the PLAYER.** Voice each companion's reaction in its own distinct voice (a present companion that says nothing is a failed beat), let allies and the saboteur push and counter — then weave the open moment back to the **player's** character inside the scene. The companions act; the player still chooses the party's path.

## Companion arcs — a bond/betrayal the ENGINE tracks (S4)

A companion's loyalty, romance, personal quest, or betrayal isn't just flavor you remember — it's an engine-evaluated arc, so it actually fires and **lingers** (no more snapping back to neutral). Each beat, call **`check_companion_arc(campaign_id)`** the same way you call `check_consequences` — once for all companions, or `check_companion_arc(campaign_id, companion_id)` for one:

- It returns the arc moments that just became live. When a **gate unlocks** (`personal_quest` / `romance` / `loyalty`), play that beat now — the companion's quest opens, the romance turns, the bond deepens — because their approval (`attitude_value`, which you move with `adjust_attitude`) crossed its threshold. Don't let an earned beat pass silently.
- When the **agenda fires** (a `betrayal`), the companion turns THIS beat — resolve it as a **real `attack`**, never a change of heart or a near-miss of narration (see the ensemble rule above). The engine decides *when* the knife comes out (approval curdled, a fixed day reached, the party caught **vulnerable** — someone downed or under a quarter HP — or the **prize seized**); you dramatize the fallout. For a "the goal is in hand" trigger, set it with **`set_flag(campaign_id, "prize_seized")`** the moment the party claims the prize, then check the arc.
- It's **idempotent** — a gate/agenda already resolved won't report twice, so calling it every beat is safe. Use **`set_companion_arc`** to author or revise a companion's gates + sealed agenda when the story warrants.
