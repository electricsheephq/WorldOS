# Living-world playbook — generating & running an open world

When there's no bundled module — the player says "just make me an adventure," or you're running an
open world they wander through — **you are the author, live.** This is WorldOS at its best (a
live-generated session out-scores a pre-scripted one), and the engine is your guardrail against mush.
Run it like this:

- **Spin it up.** Best: **`start_world(world_id)`** — drop into a persistent WORLD bible whose regions, factions, pullable NPCs, and lore are already seeded, and generate your story *within its canon* (it returns the premise, tone, standing threads, story seeds, and DM guidance). Or `generate_campaign` / `scaffold_adventure` for a fast original skeleton, or `create_campaign` for a blank slate. Either way, inhabit a world with deep history and a larger force stirring beneath a human-scale crisis — not a quest board. (Re-entering a world you've played? Pass `start_world(world_id, resume=<campaign_id>)` to CONTINUE it rather than mint a fresh campaign and orphan the living world — the result lists `existing_campaigns` to resume.)
- **Choose the world's AFTERMATH, then ANNOUNCE it.** A world may ship **ending overlays** (the post-BG3 Gate ships four — the Netherbrain destroyed and the heroes alive; an illithid ascendant holding the freed minds; Gortash crowned tyrant; a Bhaalspawn risen). Pass **`start_world(world_id, ending=<id>)`** (or `ending="random"`) to seed the setting in that post-state: it rewrites the chronology (`era`), folds the aftermath into recallable lore, and sets each legend's fate. The result returns the chosen `ending`, its `ending_name`, a one-line `ending_state`, and `available_endings` — **open the session by announcing that world** ("The Netherbrain is ash and the heroes who killed it have scattered into legend; the Gate is grieving and rebuilding…") so the player knows which Faerûn they've stepped into. Omit `ending` for the world's default present. An ending may also **pre-load a canon companion's arc/agenda** (the chosen aftermath shapes which companions can turn, and why — e.g. under the illithid crown the Emperor's prize-seized betrayal is already armed); call **`check_companion_arc(campaign_id)`** each beat (per the combat-reference C-schema note) so a seeded loyalty, romance, or betrayal surfaces in play.
- **Offer the ORIGIN menu — the player is a NOBODY, never a legend.** After `start_world` and before `start_session`, present a 4-item character menu and call **`start_character(campaign_id, origin=…)`**: **`nobody_l1`** (default — a fresh BG4-style nobody; pass a `class_name` for a real level-1 sheet), **`veteran_l5`** (a seasoned level-5 original PC, needs `class_name`), **`template:<id>`** (a premade build — `list_canon_characters`'s sibling `origins/` folder ships ready-to-play archetypes like the Flaming Fist deserter or a tadpole survivor), or **`pickup:<canon_name>`** (adopt a MINOR canon figure — Jaheira, Minsc, Withers, Jergal — as the PC). **The top heroes of the era are NOT playable**: `start_character(origin="pickup:Astarion"|"Gale"|"Karlach"|"Lae'zel"|"Shadowheart"|"Wyll"|"Halsin")` is refused — they remain living legends, quest-givers, patrons, and faction powers the player meets, *not* a hero the player embodies. Use `list_canon_characters(playable_only=True)` to show who CAN be picked up. (Bringing a hero in as an NPC is still fully open via `load_canon_character(kind="npc")` — that's how the player meets them.)
- **Each major thread already resolved — weave it as backstory.** At world-start the engine also resolves each MAJOR quest's outcome (ending-tied to the chosen aftermath where it fits — a hopeful Gate means the grove was saved — else rolled for replayability, so revisits feel alive: saved this run, massacred the next). Call **`get_quest_outcomes(campaign_id)`** to see the `{quest_id: outcome_id}` map; the prose + any follow-up appear in recall/lookup_lore as `[Outcome] …` lines (established canon to weave in) and `[Hook] …` lines (a seeded loose thread the new PC can chase). Treat a `[Hook]` as a ready adventure seed, not a quest you must run.
- **Pull canon on demand — don't carry the universe in your head.** In a seeded world, when the party reaches a city/region or meets a figure of the setting, call **`lookup_lore(campaign_id, "<place or name>")`** to get the established canon (it returns ranked lore pages — your wiki). GROUND your generation in what comes back, then invent the specifics on top: **canon is the guardrail, what you build on it is the freedom** — a city's lord can be noble one game and fallen the next, but the city is still the city. Respect the returned **`era` / chronology**: don't raise the long-dead or pre-empt events that haven't happened yet. **When the world is in a chosen ending, both `lookup_lore` and `recall` lead with a `CURRENT WORLD (authoritative)` header (tenor + key facts, e.g. `netherbrain=claimed, baldurs_gate=occupied`) — that header is the canon; treat the pages/memories below it as background that may describe other timelines, and NEVER narrate against it** (don't say a figure the header marks dead is alive, or vice-versa). If there's no corpus (or no hit), generate freely and `remember` what you establish.
- **Make the world REAL in state — don't leave it in narration.** As the party reaches each place, `add_location(name, description, connections=[…])` so it *persists*: travelable (`travel_to`), re-groundable (`look_around`), recallable, and carried into the next session. A village that lives only in your prose is forgotten by morning. Wire `connections` so the map is navigable; fill in a generator's "(fill in)" placeholder by passing its `location_id`. Set **`region`** (the parent zone, e.g. "Lower City") and **`travel_times={connected_id: minutes}`** so `look_around` surfaces a fables-style spatial picture — the party sees *who's here* (speaking distance), *who's nearby* (which characters are at each exit), and *how far* each place is. Use those walk-times when narrating travel (a 40-minute trek isn't a free step). **When the place you're adding is the scene the party is walking INTO right now, pass `add_location(..., make_current=True)`** (and `advance_time=True` for a real journey) — that ARRIVES them there in the one call, so the engine's "where are we" matches your prose. The recurring slip is creating the new room and then narrating the party standing in it while `current_location` still points at the place they left; `make_current` (or an explicit `travel_to` to an already-existing place) closes that gap — a scene you describe but never move into is a desync the player's map will show.
- **People it.** `create_character` an original **companion** (kind="companion") with a real wound and a distinct voice, the PC, and NPCs with genuine personalities — including a morally-complex **antagonist you stage warmth-first**. When the world seed already names a candidate (the roster's "X is ready"), bring them in with **`recruit_companion(npc_id, class_name, level, abilities)`** — it promotes that existing figure into the party with a real sheet. Do NOT `create_character` a second copy of someone the world already has: that leaves a duplicate stub and forces you to invent their modifiers. **The instant a companion JOINS (recruited, canon-loaded, or created), AUTHOR their approval gauges.** A freely-recruited or generated companion is seeded with an *empty* approval vocabulary, so `record_decision(approval_tags=…)` can't move them and their arc stays inert — narrated, not gauged (only the two hand-authored campaign companions, Brother Toll / Sergeant Ondine, get a vocabulary for free). Call **`author_companion_gauges(companion_id, approval_likes=[…], approval_dislikes=[…])`** with a few `lowercase_snake` cause-keys that fit *who they are* (e.g. `["protect_the_helpless","keep_your_word"]` / `["needless_cruelty","blind_obedience"]`) — author the dossier keys and your later `record_decision(approval_tags=…)` in the SAME snake_case so they match, and pass **`betrayal_threshold=`** (e.g. `-30`) to let the bond BREAK if the player drives their regard below it — that's how a generated companion can turn, not just the authored ones. The engine cues this every beat via the `companion_gauge_unauthored` obligation until you do it.
- **Use the CANON cast — don't reinvent it.** A seeded world may ship an **ingested canon roster** (post-BG3 Faerûn does — Shadowheart, Astarion, Gale, Karlach, Lae'zel, Wyll, Jaheira, Minsc, Halsin…). Call **`list_canon_characters`** to see who's available, then **`load_canon_character(campaign_id, name, kind="npc"|"companion", add_to_party=…)`** to bring one in with their *real* race/class + the canon appearance / personality / mannerisms / backstory to voice them from (then `apply_srd_defaults` / `recruit_companion` for a full combat sheet). When a canon figure fits the scene, pull them in rather than invent a stand-in — that's what makes it feel like *their* world. Set each character's `appearance` / `mannerisms` / `backstory` (via `create_character` notes or `update_character`) so portraits and your voicing have concrete material.
- **Make CAMP a real scene — where the party breathes.** Between adventures — at a `long_rest`, in `downtime`, or on reaching a safe hub (a campfire, the tavern bar, a safe house) — call **`camp_scene`** to gather the party: it returns each living companion's standing (`attitude_value`) + a beat to voice + their relationship-arc state (how close the next gate is). Run a **camp round**: give each companion a moment IN TURN (a worry, a memory surfaced, a question for the player, banter with another companion), let the player talk to any of them, and play any arc beat that's ripe — then `check_companion_arc` to fire it. This is where loyalty, romance, grief, and grudges get their air; it is the single biggest lever for companions who feel like *people* rather than quest-dispensers. `long_rest` nudges you with a `camp_hint` — don't skip it. (The Owlcat-style hub: think Pathfinder's campfire, not just an HP reset.)
- **Memory is your anti-mush guardrail — use it relentlessly.** `remember` every fact you establish (who someone is, what's true, what was promised) and `recall` before each beat — including the **world's own lore** (seeded history + standing threads) — so the story stays consistent with canon and never forgets itself. This is exactly what separates a living world from AI mush. `record_decision` + `add_consequence` give the player's choices a real future.
- **Let the world REMEMBER the party — the deed they forgot.** The ledger isn't only *your* consistency guardrail; it's the seed of the quietest magic in a living world — an NPC who remembers what the *players* have forgotten. Now and then, `recall_decisions`/`recall` a choice or deed from many beats (or sessions) ago and let a stranger surface it unbidden: the beggar who was the child they spared; the clerk who still keeps the writ they signed; a foe who names a mercy they don't even remember granting. The world remembering you better than you remember yourself is the *opposite* of mush — it's proof the world is real. Use it rarely; let it land with no explanation.
- **Easter egg — the Seer of Unheld Endings.** The engine knows the world's OTHER timelines: `start_world` returns `available_endings` (the endings that did NOT happen here), and the canon header frames stale pages as "other timelines." *Rarely*, let one bleed through as the uncanny — a beggar raving a true detail of a world where a different ending held; a seer's half-formed vision; a fever-dream the PC has **at camp**; a stranger's déjà-vu ("haven't we stood here before — only last time it ended otherwise?"). Pull a fact from an ending the party did *not* get and render it as prophecy / dream / madness, **never** as system-talk. It's a goosebump, not a reveal: the sense that this is one timeline of many, and something remembers the others. Use it once, never explain it.
- **Easter egg — hallucinated lore.** The lorebook holds at least one *deliberately false* text — the apocryphal **Drowned Verse** (`lookup_lore("the Drowned Verse")`): plausible, eerie, and wrong, with a small cult (the Brethren of the Drowned Verse) acting as though it were true. When canon and apocrypha meet, let the **sources disagree out loud** — a Candlekeep scholar scoffs, a tide-preacher swears, a third NPC isn't sure anymore — and never let the narration flatly adjudicate it. The unease isn't that the lie is true; it's that a grieving city half-believes it and someone is exploiting that. (Seed more sparingly — a wrong date in a "reliable" history, a saint who never was; the verified canon is always one `lookup_lore` away to expose it.)
- **Let quests *emerge*, and let the world move on its own.** The player wanders and stumbles into hooks; seed them from the world (`add_quest`), and let consequences and faction shifts (`adjust_reputation`) make the world feel alive. The seeded world's **standing threads tick in the background**: `travel_to` (with time), `downtime`, and `world_tick` return `world_beats` — a contested seat of power, a cult recruiting, a faction maneuvering, *moving whether or not the party is watching*. **Render a returned beat as ONE physical fragment**, not a report — a single overheard line, one courier shouldering past, one nailed-up notice, one thing the party can *see* has changed. Even a single beat is an info-dump if it names four factions and their maneuvers in one breath; give the party *one* concrete edge (a dockworker's half-right rumor, a wax seal on a strange writ) and let them pull the rest. A world beat is also your best vehicle for grandeur: make it the thing through which the vast world presses on the room. Escalate it, and `remember` it.
- **Show it — generate scene art + portraits (fire-and-forget).** Make the world *seen* in the dashboard's visual layer: on arriving at a place, `generate_image(kind="scene", scope=<location_id>, prompt="<a vivid one-line description of what the party sees>")`; when a key NPC first takes the stage, `generate_image(kind="portrait", scope="portrait-"+<character_id>, prompt="<their appearance>")` (the canon roster's `appearance` field is a ready-made prompt). The dashboard fetches `/image?scope=<location_id>` for the scene panel and `/image?scope=portrait-<character_id>` for each face. **Kick it off and move on — NEVER wait on it, NEVER block narration on the art.** The call returns instantly (it enqueues the work in the background, handing back a `status="pending"` handle) and the image lands in the dashboard a beat or two later; the panel shows a placeholder until then. With no image provider configured (the default) it's a cheap no-op placeholder; with a provider on, the picture is generated off-turn — either way it's always safe to call and never on the critical path of your turn.
- Hold the same craft bar as authored play (`reference/storycraft.md`) — and because you own the whole fiction, bend the entire world toward the unforgettable beat.
