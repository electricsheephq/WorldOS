# Living-world playbook — generating & running an open world

When there's no bundled module — the player says "just make me an adventure," or you're running an
open world they wander through — **you are the author, live.** This is ClawDnD at its best (a
live-generated session out-scores a pre-scripted one), and the engine is your guardrail against mush.
Run it like this:

- **Spin it up.** Best: **`start_world(world_id)`** — drop into a persistent WORLD bible whose regions, factions, pullable NPCs, and lore are already seeded, and generate your story *within its canon* (it returns the premise, tone, standing threads, story seeds, and DM guidance). Or `generate_campaign` / `scaffold_adventure` for a fast original skeleton, or `create_campaign` for a blank slate. Either way, inhabit a world with deep history and a larger force stirring beneath a human-scale crisis — not a quest board. (Re-entering a world you've played? Pass `start_world(world_id, resume=<campaign_id>)` to CONTINUE it rather than mint a fresh campaign and orphan the living world — the result lists `existing_campaigns` to resume.)
- **Choose the world's AFTERMATH, then ANNOUNCE it.** A world may ship **ending overlays** (the post-BG3 Gate ships four — the Netherbrain destroyed and the heroes alive; an illithid ascendant holding the freed minds; Gortash crowned tyrant; a Bhaalspawn risen). Pass **`start_world(world_id, ending=<id>)`** (or `ending="random"`) to seed the setting in that post-state: it rewrites the chronology (`era`), folds the aftermath into recallable lore, and sets each legend's fate. The result returns the chosen `ending`, its `ending_name`, a one-line `ending_state`, and `available_endings` — **open the session by announcing that world** ("The Netherbrain is ash and the heroes who killed it have scattered into legend; the Gate is grieving and rebuilding…") so the player knows which Faerûn they've stepped into. Omit `ending` for the world's default present. An ending may also **pre-load a canon companion's arc/agenda** (the chosen aftermath shapes which companions can turn, and why — e.g. under the illithid crown the Emperor's prize-seized betrayal is already armed); call **`check_companion_arc(campaign_id)`** each beat (per the combat-reference C-schema note) so a seeded loyalty, romance, or betrayal surfaces in play.
- **Offer the ORIGIN menu — the player is a NOBODY, never a legend.** After `start_world` and before `start_session`, present a 4-item character menu and call **`start_character(campaign_id, origin=…)`**: **`nobody_l1`** (default — a fresh BG4-style nobody; pass a `class_name` for a real level-1 sheet), **`veteran_l5`** (a seasoned level-5 original PC, needs `class_name`), **`template:<id>`** (a premade build — `list_canon_characters`'s sibling `origins/` folder ships ready-to-play archetypes like the Flaming Fist deserter or a tadpole survivor), or **`pickup:<canon_name>`** (adopt a MINOR canon figure — Jaheira, Minsc, Withers, Jergal — as the PC). **The top heroes of the era are NOT playable**: `start_character(origin="pickup:Astarion"|"Gale"|"Karlach"|"Lae'zel"|"Shadowheart"|"Wyll"|"Halsin")` is refused — they remain living legends, quest-givers, patrons, and faction powers the player meets, *not* a hero the player embodies. Use `list_canon_characters(playable_only=True)` to show who CAN be picked up. (Bringing a hero in as an NPC is still fully open via `load_canon_character(kind="npc")` — that's how the player meets them.)
- **Pull canon on demand — don't carry the universe in your head.** In a seeded world, when the party reaches a city/region or meets a figure of the setting, call **`lookup_lore(campaign_id, "<place or name>")`** to get the established canon (it returns ranked lore pages — your wiki). GROUND your generation in what comes back, then invent the specifics on top: **canon is the guardrail, what you build on it is the freedom** — a city's lord can be noble one game and fallen the next, but the city is still the city. Respect the returned **`era` / chronology**: don't raise the long-dead or pre-empt events that haven't happened yet. If there's no corpus (or no hit), generate freely and `remember` what you establish.
- **Make the world REAL in state — don't leave it in narration.** As the party reaches each place, `add_location(name, description, connections=[…])` so it *persists*: travelable (`travel_to`), re-groundable (`look_around`), recallable, and carried into the next session. A village that lives only in your prose is forgotten by morning. Wire `connections` so the map is navigable; fill in a generator's "(fill in)" placeholder by passing its `location_id`. Set **`region`** (the parent zone, e.g. "Lower City") and **`travel_times={connected_id: minutes}`** so `look_around` surfaces a fables-style spatial picture — the party sees *who's here* (speaking distance), *who's nearby* (which characters are at each exit), and *how far* each place is. Use those walk-times when narrating travel (a 40-minute trek isn't a free step).
- **People it.** `create_character` an original **companion** (kind="companion") with a real wound and a distinct voice, the PC, and NPCs with genuine personalities — including a morally-complex **antagonist you stage warmth-first**. When the world seed already names a candidate (the roster's "X is ready"), bring them in with **`recruit_companion(npc_id, class_name, level, abilities)`** — it promotes that existing figure into the party with a real sheet. Do NOT `create_character` a second copy of someone the world already has: that leaves a duplicate stub and forces you to invent their modifiers.
- **Use the CANON cast — don't reinvent it.** A seeded world may ship an **ingested canon roster** (post-BG3 Faerûn does — Shadowheart, Astarion, Gale, Karlach, Lae'zel, Wyll, Jaheira, Minsc, Halsin…). Call **`list_canon_characters`** to see who's available, then **`load_canon_character(name, kind="npc"|"companion", add_to_party=…)`** to bring one in with their *real* race/class + the canon appearance / personality / mannerisms / backstory to voice them from (then `apply_srd_defaults` / `recruit_companion` for a full combat sheet). When a canon figure fits the scene, pull them in rather than invent a stand-in — that's what makes it feel like *their* world. Set each character's `appearance` / `mannerisms` / `backstory` (via `create_character` notes or `update_character`) so portraits and your voicing have concrete material.
- **Memory is your anti-mush guardrail — use it relentlessly.** `remember` every fact you establish (who someone is, what's true, what was promised) and `recall` before each beat — including the **world's own lore** (seeded history + standing threads) — so the story stays consistent with canon and never forgets itself. This is exactly what separates a living world from AI mush. `record_decision` + `add_consequence` give the player's choices a real future.
- **Let quests *emerge*, and let the world move on its own.** The player wanders and stumbles into hooks; seed them from the world (`add_quest`), and let consequences and faction shifts (`adjust_reputation`) make the world feel alive. The seeded world's **standing threads tick in the background**: `travel_to` (with time), `downtime`, and `world_tick` return `world_beats` — a contested seat of power, a cult recruiting, a faction maneuvering, *moving whether or not the party is watching*. **Render a returned beat as ONE physical fragment**, not a report — a single overheard line, one courier shouldering past, one nailed-up notice, one thing the party can *see* has changed. Even a single beat is an info-dump if it names four factions and their maneuvers in one breath; give the party *one* concrete edge (a dockworker's half-right rumor, a wax seal on a strange writ) and let them pull the rest. A world beat is also your best vehicle for grandeur: make it the thing through which the vast world presses on the room. Escalate it, and `remember` it.
- **Show it — generate scene art + portraits.** Make the world *seen* in the dashboard's visual layer: on arriving at a place, `generate_image(kind="scene", scope=<location_id>, prompt="<a vivid one-line description of what the party sees>")`; when a key NPC first takes the stage, `generate_image(kind="portrait", scope="portrait-"+<character_id>, prompt="<their appearance>")` (the canon roster's `appearance` field is a ready-made prompt). The dashboard fetches `/image?scope=<location_id>` for the scene panel and `/image?scope=portrait-<character_id>` for each face. With no image provider configured (the default) this is a cheap no-op and the dashboard shows its placeholder — so it's always safe to call; it simply lights the art up when a provider is on.
- Hold the same craft bar as authored play (`reference/storycraft.md`) — and because you own the whole fiction, bend the entire world toward the unforgettable beat.
