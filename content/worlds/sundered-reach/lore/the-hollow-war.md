# The Hollow War

*Era: The war ended ~12 years ago; this is the history every living adult in the Reach carries. Keep it past, not present.*

The Hollow War is the wound the whole Reach is built around. It began when the death-cult called the **Pale Choir** completed a working beneath the city of **Vael's Crossing** and tore open the **Sundering** — a rift to a devouring **Silence** that did not kill so much as *unmake*. Where the Silence touched, land went grey and wrong, the dead did not stay dead in any way worth the word, and — worst, and least spoken of — **memory itself thinned**: names, faces, whole years gone from the people who survived, as if the Silence ate backward through what had been.

The free cities had never agreed on anything. They agreed on this. In desperation they forged the **Ashen Concord** — a wartime council of rivals — and spent a generation of young soldiers holding the Silence back from the coast while their scholars and wardens searched for a way to close the rift.

There was only one. The rift could be sealed, but only from beneath the floodwaters — by **drowning Vael's Crossing** whole, and everything the Silence had not yet taken with it. The order was given by the **Vael line itself**, the city's own rulers. Thousands went under: refugees who had fled there for safety, and the Concord's own defenders who could not be pulled back in time. The sea closed over the spires and the Silence guttered out.

The Reach calls it **the Drowning**, and it has never decided whether the word means *salvation* or *atrocity*. It means both. That unresolved double-meaning is the moral key of the entire setting: victory here already cost too much, every faction has a real and bloody point, and the people who saved the world are the same people who drowned a city of their own.

Twelve years on, the seal holds beneath the waves at low tide — and pilgrims, oracles, and frightened children say it is beginning to **thin**. Most of the Concord calls that grief and superstition. It is not.
