# The Powers of the Sundered Reach

*Era: twelve years after the Hollow War. Every faction has a real point; none can lead the Reach alone.*

Four powers shape the Reach. None is simply good or evil — that is the setting's register.

**The Ashen Concord** — the wartime council of free cities, forged in desperation to fight the Hollow War and slowly dying of its own rivalries ever since. It won the war and gave the order to drown Vael's Crossing; it carries both the glory and the guilt. Honorable in pieces (individual members are decent, like **Maren Vael**), sclerotic as a whole. It wants to hold the fragile peace and is too divided to do much else. Its seat is **Brassmoor**; its great unspoken fear is that the seal is failing and naming that truth would shatter the council into open war between **Brassmoor** and **Greywater**.

**The Pale Choir** — the death-cult that opened the Sundering, scattered but not destroyed, now recruiting the grieving with terrible kindness. The world's antagonist gravity. (See *The Pale Choir*.) They believe the **Silence** is mercy and work quietly to hasten the seal's thinning. Reputation across the Reach: feared and hunted — but their preachers are loved by the people they tend, which is exactly the problem.

**The Lantern Order** — a battered warden-order sworn to watch the seal at Vael's Crossing and bury its drowned. Honest, zealous, under-resourced, and quietly terrified they cannot hold what they guard. They are merciful toward the Choir's *pawns* (the grieving are victims, in their view) and iron-hard about the Silence itself. The companion **Castellan Bram Oye** is one of theirs. The Order is the closest thing the Reach has to a conscience — and it is exhausted.

**The Greywater League** — the merchant-princes of the neutral free-port of **Greywater**, who found profit in the ruins and sell to every side at once. Charming, ruthless, superbly informed (their spymaster **Vance Greywater** knows everyone's secrets and prices them by the ounce). They are not villains: sometimes the League is the only power that actually *gets things done*, and they genuinely love the Reach — the way a man loves a thing he means to own.

The political truth of the Reach: the war is won, the peace is rotting, and no power trusts another to hold the lantern. Into that gap the player steps.
