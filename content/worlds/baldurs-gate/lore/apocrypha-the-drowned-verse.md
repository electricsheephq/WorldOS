# The Drowned Verse (apocryphal)

*Era: disputed — circulated in the months after the fall of the Absolute, 1492 DR.*

**This is an APOCRYPHAL text — a disputed, almost-certainly-false document.** Candlekeep's
sages catalogue it under *hoaxes and grief-born heresies* and will say so to anyone who asks. It
is recorded here because people *believe* it, not because it is true — and belief, in a wounded
city, is its own kind of force.

## The claim

The Drowned Verse holds that the Netherbrain was **not wholly destroyed** in the harbor. It teaches
that the Absolute's mind was *two*, a twin coiled inside the first, and that when the great brain
died the lesser one **sank** rather than burned — that it lies now in the cold dark beneath the
Gray Harbor mud, dreaming, and that on a certain low tide it will "sing the drowned awake." The
text is written as a liturgy: tide-tables, a call-and-response, a list of "the names the water
keeps."

It is wrong. The canon record is plain — the Netherbrain breached the Lower City and was destroyed
in the harbor; there was no twin; the scar in the streets is the end of that story, not a prologue.
A reader who pulls the histories alongside this verse will find them flatly disagreeing, and the
sages siding with the histories.

## The Brethren of the Drowned Verse

A small cult has formed around it all the same — dockworkers, tadpole-survivors who cannot believe
the nightmare simply *ended*, a defrocked acolyte or two. The **Brethren of the Drowned Verse**
gather at slack tide on the harbor stairs, recite the names the water keeps, and wait. Most are
harmless and grieving. A few are not: someone is *funding* them, someone has begun adding real
names to the liturgy, and a Brethren cell has started "helping the song along" — which is where a
party tends to come in.

## Running it (DM)

Treat this as the **hallucinated-lore easter egg**: a thread of plausible, eerie *falsehood* the
world half-believes. Surface the verse via `lookup_lore` like any page — but when the party digs,
let the sources **disagree out loud** (a Candlekeep-trained scholar scoffs; a Brethren preacher
swears on the tide; a third NPC isn't sure anymore), and never have the narration adjudicate it
flatly. The horror isn't that the twin is real — it's that you can't *prove* it isn't, and a cult
is acting as though it were. Whether anything actually stirs under the harbor is yours to decide
per campaign; the verse itself is, and remains, apocryphal.
