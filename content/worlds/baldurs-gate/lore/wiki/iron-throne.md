# Iron Throne

The **Iron Throne**, or simply **the Throne**, was a mysterious mercantile organization that operated out of the Eastern Heartlands during the 14 century DR. Though it controlled much of the weapons trade across the Realms during its time, its leadership and hierarchy remained unknown to those outside its ranks. While it was a criminal organization in every sense of the word, the Iron Throne thrived in part because of the façade it projected to the rest of Faerûn: that of an honorable business that served the best interests of the Realms. 

## Reputation
The Iron Throne was a subject of mystery and conjecture, with little if anything known of its aims or even its leaders. It wasn't even known where it began or was based, beyond being somewhere in the Eastern Heartlands; some assumed it originated in Sembia. Efforts to divine their identities or plans through magic had all failed as of 1367 DR. Because so little was known about the Iron Throne's leaders, many speculated about their identities. Some believed them to be dwarven nobles removed from their seats of power; elven renegades; agents of the nation of Thay, of the kingdom of Cormyr, or even the Zhentarim of Zhentil Keep, and that any conflict with those states was for misdirection. There were unconfirmed reports of undead beholders, intelligent lizards, and pale-blue giants that came from the sea. Some even thought a deity like Cyric or Leira was somehow involved or a dead god was using them as a tool to seek resurrection. Another recurring rumor suspected many Waukeenar priests or even the whole church was closely associated. By 1367 DR, it had at least been determined that it was not a front for the Zhentarim, despite their dealings, owing to their conflicts. This rumor had been put about by merchants negatively impacted by the Iron Throne's conduct. The Zhents in turn falsely suspected it was funded by Cormyr to compete with their Black Network.

## Organization
### Structure
Overall, the Iron Throne had a segmented hierarchy, with its lower levels divided into branches that operated independently and even unaware of the others, in high secrecy. The full scope of their collective activities was known only by the Iron Throne's leader and their inner circle. The rank-and-file were informed only of what they were required to know.

The leadership of the Iron Throne comprised 'the Head' of the organization, Sfena, a tiefling thief under a bizarre curse and left as little more than a crystallized bones, and an inner circle of three adjutants, known unofficially as advisors, all of them chosen personally by Sfena. These officers were each responsible for a different aspect of the Iron Throne's operations. They were:
- 'The Arm': A former assassin Ritchar, the Red Man, this individual arranged top-level mercantile contracts, organized the routes they used for trade, and handled their smuggling rings, as well as monitored trade in surrounding lands and identified ideal times to strike rival merchants.
- 'The Foot': Filled by the disguised storm giant Krakosh, this officer was responsible for protecting the organization's caravans and interfering with those belonging to other trading groups. He saw to the hiring and training of caravan guards for the former and recruited criminals and bandits for the latter.
- 'The Eye': The half-elf mage Maready acted as a magical coordinator for the inner circle. He facilitated communication between members through both magical and mundane means and gathered information by means of divination.

These three individuals were each served in turn by three lesser officers, known formally as Lesser Arms, Lesser Feet, and Lesser Eyes and informally as lesser advisors. They were more involved with day-to-day operations of their divisions and managing contacts with the lower ranks. Typically, they were the highest-ranked members of the Iron Throne known to its merchants and mercenary leaders, and none outside the organization knew how many there were; at least five was the common estimate. Each lesser advisor was assigned to a particular region. Sfena approved any appointments and changes to these positions and would expand their number if the Iron Throne grew enough to require it. She had each of them swallow one of her bones too.

All of the advisors and lesser advisors had each swallowed one of Sfena's own crystallized bones, which shielded them against divinations that might reveal their auras, thoughts, or true identities. Furthermore, to prevent dissension amongst the group's senior members, they were each placed under the effects of a *geas* spell by Sfena.

Following Sfena's disappearance in 1371 DR, Krakosh took charge of the Iron Throne and, following a brief conflict between the advisors, emerged as the leader with Maready as his aide-de-camp.

### Membership
Subordinate to the lesser advisors was a wide assortment of common merchants, craftsmen, drovers, caravan masters, and caravan guards for the Iron Throne's legitimate businesses, and of course the rather-less-common adventurers, bandits, brutes, criminals, saboteurs, spies, assassins, outcasts, and other dregs of society for its illegitimate businesses. Either way, such folk came from all trades and classes and from most of the common races of Faerûn, though of course the criminal element largely came from the lower cases. They had no special titles or status.

They were divided into three divisions according to their role and whom they served: the **Arms**, the **Feet**, and the **Eyes**. Smooth-talking negotiators and those with a mind for business were often recruited to serve the Arm, while all manner of sell-swords and criminals were hired to fight under the Foot. Meanwhile, the Eye's agents included other spellcasters like wizards or clerics or individuals with a strong network of informants. They mostly acted independently from one another, but when working with members outside their division but of equivalent rank, those of the Feet usually deferred to those of the Arm, and those of the Arms in turn usually deferred to those of the Eyes. In normal circumstances, they had no knowledge of nor contact with other Iron Throne members except those with whom they worked directly, usually within a merchant house or guild, mercenary force, bandit gang, or hired group of diviners. The leaders of such groups were unaware of other groups even within their division, but were in contact with a lesser advisor or intermediary who had some knowledge of other group leaders. Any communication with the leadership was through them or through anonymous messengers. Only lesser advisors knew all the groups and their leaders in their division and region, and knew of a handful of others outside it so they could ally or muster a large team in a pinch. And almost no one knew of Sfena outside her inner circle, let alone her links to the Nine Hells.

The organization numbered 3,530 people in 1372 DR. Morally, they ran a gamut from lawful to evil to chaotic, but not good. The Iron Throne did not venerate any one god, nor serve their interests, but Mask, Waukeen, Shar, and even Kelemvor were favored by many members. They were united solely by their avariciousness, and some by their ambition, of which the most capable would rise highest in the Iron Throne.

### Recruitment
Although the Iron Throne insisted its members were but honest merchants acting per the letter of the law, almost all of them were in fact schemers, thugs, and bandits. They were unscrupulous, manipulative, treacherous, and opportunistic, but committed, and were willing to break deals and betray short-term alliances for profit, political influence, or simple convenience, much like their mistress Sfena, who actually selected employees as amoral as herself. Her one requirement was maintaining the façade of civil and legal behavior like her baatezu ancestors, however superficial it might be, and she would terminate any immediate employee who broke this code. They also went out of their way to hire adventurers.

Naturally, the turnover rate for the ranks of brutes, bandits, and assassins was fairly rapid as, whenever they were caught performing illegal acts, the Iron Throne swiftly disavowed and expelled them for "acting without permission" or reverting to their former ways. Hence, most were newly hired, or newly fired. They would freely hire all kinds of people as cat's-paws.
