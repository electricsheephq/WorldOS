# Maglubiyet

**Maglubiyet** (), known as **The Mighty One** or **The High Chieftain** was the chief deity of the goblinoids and ruler of the goblinoid pantheon, lording over mortal heroes and immortal gods alike with an iron fist. Maglubiyet was truly the **Conquering God**, for he took many races and deities, once and indeed still distinct in tendency, outlook, culture, and faith, and made them one, setting them marching to his beat in service to his purpose, the purpose of conquest itself. For those laid low by the infamous **Battle Lord**, as so many others had been, there was but one choice left to make: break, or bend; die, or serve;  fall beneath his bloody axe, or bow before his iron throne.

## Description
Maglubiyet appeared as a terrifying, battle-scarred  tall goblinoid entity (be it a goblin or hobgoblin). He had ebony black skin, glowing red eyes from which red flames licked, flickered, sputtered, and erupted, sharp fangs, and muscular arms ending in vicious talons.

### Manifestations
Omens from Maglubiyet took the form of blood seeping from an axe's edge, unusual behavior from the wolves and worgs amid the mounts of the goblins. He dominated his followers through visions sent to his shamans, or sometimes the clan chieftains themselves, and might directly communicate to the goblins through the shamans through utterances spoken in trances. Maglubiyet's goblin acolytes also sought signs of his favor in other ways, constantly looking out for his approval in forms such as auspicious rolls of thunder or the nearby landing of a bird of prey.

When hobgoblins encountered other goblinoids, whether on purpose or by accident, it was viewed as a divine sign by all that Maglubiyet had called them together to do his bidding on a grand scale. When a gathered host began to fail and fall to infighting, bugbears took the disarray as a sign that Maglubiyet was no longer looking, and would soon turn on their hobgoblin leaders (taking a few of their heads as trophies) before leaving.

## Personality
In many ways, Maglubiyet was a power similar to the orc god Gruumsh. For one, like Gruumsh, his alignment was difficult to define, for he was both an iron-fisted tyrant and a whimsical, capricious ruler prone to changing his mind. Both gods eternally urged their people on to conflict and strife, wishing to see war waged for their people's glory and for them to wipe out the surface races. The difference between them laid primarily in their motivation.

Where Gruumsh sent his people to war primarily in the name of seizing new territory, Maglubiyet's missions of conquest and devastation were simply for the sake of the glory of war, battle and carnage itself. War and destruction were Maglubiyet's true passions, and though he tolerated no dissent between the goblins and hobgoblins that worked against their mutual expansion or survival, it fell to their specific racial gods to actually push for such things. Transgressions by even the devout might be punished with horrible curses until they caused enough harm to compensate for their offenses, assuming he did not consider them so severe as to simply subject the soul to eternal torture.

Maglubiyet was a truly intimidating figure, commanding fear despite being utterly devoid of charisma even by the reckoning of goblinkind. At the same time, while not truly fearful, he was also a paranoid deity. He tolerated no infringements on his power, nor for others in his pantheon to rise to true power of their own. He was one to crush all interlopers, and such was his paranoia that he found interlopers everywhere.

Maglubiyet was not just a suspicious deity, but also legendarily treacherous. He taught his people, if anything, to be self-serving, always looking out for themselves before anyone else. The Conquering God made them what they were in modern times, and with his "lessons" instilled, they had become as nasty, as grasping, and otherwise as "goblin-like" as their deity.

## Powers
A powerful war god and great general, Maglubiyet created avatars of dangerous physical strength, speed, and endurance. Swinging his axe at his foes, his sweeping arcs could strike multiple enemies at once, and those who fought back could expect a vengeful slash in return. This was assuming he did not simply hack them apart with his claws, knocking them to the ground or taking them violently into his clutches. And when he was wounded, the rage he entered made him even more dangerous, if easier to strike. The god of the goblins was also known to take on the qualities of his people, such as the gritty resilience of the hobgoblins.

Besides their physical prowess, Maglubiyet's avatars possessed a host of supernatural abilities. Not only could they resist fire and easily withstand the scorch of radiant light, they could project fire as through the *burning hands* spell, and unleash unholy fire to weaken and blind their targets. By speaking an *unholy word*, they could kill any significantly injured foe near them instantly, with only the mightiest of monsters and champions able to survive the mental pain. And should an avatar be slain, they would explode in a *fireball* of unholy vengeance, destroying those nearby with flame and psychic power.

Beyond the more specific abilities, avatars of Maglubiyet, had access to all clerical spells. They could also teleport and accelerate with the *haste* spell once per day, and would slow the injured down with their deathly aura, rendering them incapable of escape.

### Tactics
Maglubiyet usually preferred to unleash fire on his enemies as he approached before chopping and shredding them when he got into melee, although he was also known to hurl the enemies he grasped at each other. Should he be surrounded by a couple of bloodied and battered foes at once, he made use of the dire power of his *unholy word*.

## Possessions
Maglubiyet's avatar fought with a huge, jagged axe, bloodied and black as coal, seemingly fully capable of cleaving the world itself in twain. It acted as a *sword of sharpness*, and those it slashed continued to take damage in perpetuity.

## Divine Realm
Maglubiyet's divine realm was Clangor, located in Avalas, the first layer of the plane of Acheron, Banehold in the World Axis cosmology, and its own separate fiendish plane in the World Tree cosmology. It was a realm of endless war where victory was all that ultimately mattered, and the surest path to victory was the strength that came through order and discipline. Physically it took the form of a cold, iron cube, thoroughly tunneled and colonized where there was not a pitted, rusted desert. At the end of each day, the blood-red sunset would herald the magical revival of all who died fighting on the fields of battle.

The primary cube face of Clangor was completely encircled by the wide, fast-moving River Lorfang. The river welled up from a spring before traveling a few miles to circle the realm, and then plunged back into the cube not far from where it rose out. Maglubiyet, Lord of Depths and Darkness, was said to live at the bottom of the plummeting waterfall where the river ended, ruling from his throne of flaming iron in a magnificently carved steel cavern that dripped with moisture. Great sacrifices were offered to him there from the top of the waterfall before every major campaign, usually against the orcs of Nishrek.

Clangor also contained the temple-city and capital fortress of Maglubiyet known as Grashmog, the Heart of Battle. About a day's march away from the main goblin city of Shetring, it was located within the interior of the Lorfang's circle, and usually far from the lines of battle. In this respect, Grashmog was surprisingly an escape from war, a quiet, unwalled, warrior's retreat and sacred place of Maglubiyet's mysteries, occupied mainly with temples, monuments, and places holy or important to the goblin god. Regardless, war still loomed in Grashmog; it served as a training camp for the elite forces of the goblin and hobgoblin army, and its clerics advised generals on upcoming battles, ruling behind every great legion.

## Activities
From his plane he called home, Maglubiyet commanded innumerable goblinoid legions, marshaling his infamous "Army of Immortals" against the slavering orc hordes in a mythic contest that had pitted the races against each other since time immemorial. The shamans of the goblins and hobgoblins claimed that Maglubiyet always won the battles of this eternal war, but the lack of permanent death in the plane allowed the enemy to always reform. In truth, both sides endlessly tried to wipe each other's troops out, but the battle was futile, neither side able to gain the upper hand.
