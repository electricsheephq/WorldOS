# Null

**Null** () was the dragon god of death, undeath, time, and fate, worshipped under two aspects, **Chronepsis** and **Falazure**.

As **Chronepsis**, known as the *Guardian of the Lost*, he traveled through time to rectify past wrongs, measured the lives of dragonkind across the multiverse with his hourglasses, and ferried the souls of dead dragons to their respective planes, granting them eternal sanctuary from the foes of their former lives.

As **Falazure** (also spelled **Faluzure**), known as the *Reaver* and the *Night Dragon*, he was revered by evil dragons and reveled in the taking of life, bestowing blessings upon those who aided his grim work. He was also regarded as the creator of the first undead dragons, such as dracoliches and vampiric dragons.

## Description
Null typically manifested as an ancient time dragon, a black dragon with iridescent scales, or an impenetrable region of blackness in the shape of a dragon. His voice was said to be the dusty croak of the undead.

## Personality
When embodying his aspect of Chronepsis, he was a dispassionate and detached observer. In this role, he passed judgment on all dragons upon their deaths, ferried their souls to their respective planes, and ensured they were no longer troubled by enemies from their mortal lives. Though he seldom intervened in the affairs of the living, he would occasionally rely on seasoned adventurers to maintain the balance of time—whether that meant recovering stolen hourglasses, hastening the demise of a troublesome wyrm, or traveling back in time to mend a past wrongdoing.

In stark contrast, when assuming his aspect of Falazure, he was haughty, fatalistic, and utterly humorless. Embracing this darker nature, he relished the taking of lives and blessed those who served him in these bloody endeavors. Though he wasn't easily angered, he harbored grudges over offenses and attacks, be they real or imagined, allowing them to eventually fester into undying hatred.

## Abilities
Null knew the future and the fate awaiting all creation at the end of time. Manifesting as an ancient time dragon, he could travel through time with the power of the *Time Gate*. Yet beyond his mastery of fate and time, legends warned that his power of death was absolute—mere contact with him would cause instant death. Worse still, his dominion over undeath twisted the shells of the creatures he slew into his undead entourage.

## Realm
In the World Tree cosmology, Null's realm occupied the peak of Dragon Eyrie and no other draconic god contested that territory.

In the Great Wheel cosmology, he had mausoleums in both Carceri and the Outlands. His neutral aspect, Chronepsis, resided in the Mausoleum of Chronepsis in the Outlands, whereas his evil aspect, Falazure, dwelt within the Mausoleum of Pain in Carceri. Rumors suggested that these two mausoleums partially coexisted in the Plane of Shadow and the Negative Energy Plane, or that they were actually a single structure spanning both the Outlands and Carceri.

Inside the Outlands Mausoleum, Chronepsis dwelt in a vast cavern beneath the ruins of a once-great city. Scattered throughout his subterranean domain were an infinite number of hourglasses, each of which was said to measure the lifespan of a member of dragonkind somewhere in the multiverse.

Meanwhile, within the Mausoleum of Pain in Carceri, Falazure lurked in his burrow, surrounded by a wasteland of bones. There, he performed vile necromantic experiments on stolen corpses, and was served by an army of juju zombies ready to slay any creature foolish enough to approach.

## Relationships
It was said that Null was the son of Asgorath and the brother of Bahamut and Tiamat.

As Chronepsis, he was dispassionate and held neither allegiances nor enemies, though Labelas Enoreth had an understanding with him.

As Falazure, he harbored a deep hatred for both Bahamut and Tiamat. Although he was once allied with Tiamat, some rift in prehistory drove them into everlasting enmity. He also nurtured a deep grudge against Hlal, who, according to myths, once played a particularly elaborate practical joke on him that robbed him of his dignity. He resented Tamara's interference with the inevitability of death as well, loathing her with a special passion. His only known ally was Kalzareinad, and the death of the demigod led scholars to speculate he might seek others to replace him.

After the Spellplague, Chronepsis served as an exarch for the Raven Queen while Falazure became a devotee of Vecna.

## Worshipers
Null's worshipers were as divergent as the deity's own dual aspects.

Under his mantle as Chronepsis, he garnered the respect of all dragons, who sometimes offered him tribute to ensure the animae of their dearly departed were shepherded safely to the afterlife. He was also revered by some non-dragon creatures like kobolds, lizardfolk, and troglodytes.

Conversely, in his aspect of Falazure, his following teemed with evil dragons, shadow dragons, and dracoliches. This malevolent influence extended far beyond dragonkind, reaching necromancers of every race and intelligent undead such as liches.

### Clergy
Followers who venerated Null and could cast priest spells served as Null's clerics, known as annihilists. The attitudes of these annihilists varied wildly depending on which aspect they worshiped.

Clerics devoted to his neutral aspect, Chronepsis, viewed other faiths with indifference, considering them neither allies nor enemies.

Followers of his evil aspect, Falazure, counted all members of good-aligned faiths as their enemies.

### Temples
Temples to Null took the form of underground caverns that were cloaked in shadows and only dimly lit. Dragons of all breeds and alignments made their way to these places before they died, and their remains lay strewn across the floor, alongside whatever treasure they had carried. The spirits of these dragons, as well as other spectral guardians, lingered to guard these temples from any who sought to steal from the burial grounds. Yet, Null's care extended beyond dragons: creatures of any kind, regardless of their alignment in life, were welcome to find eternal rest in these temples upon death.

Two Nullist centers of worship were of major significance to his followers. The Well of Dragons, where countless dragons made their final pilgrimage to meet their end, was a huge natural cauldron located due east of the Skull Gorge. The Dire Dragon—rumored to have been transformed into his undead state by Null's evil aspect, Falazure—maintained his lair within this ancient temple. The Crypt of Dragons was an underground cavern-tomb somewhere near the town of Hilp in Cormyr. This shrine was thought to be the biggest remaining temple dedicated exclusively to Null's neutral aspect, Chronepsis.

### Dogma
As Chronepsis, he taught that all life eventually led into death, which was simply a starting point to another kind existence. True death, on the other hand, was final and absolute.

As Falazure, he taught that death and decay were inevitable and omnipresent and that dragons should incorporate aspects of death into their lives in order to truly be strong. Embracing death in life heralded that one day the afterlife would incorporate the living world as well. He also encouraged his non-dragon followers to delve into undeath eternal.

### Rites
Dragon followers of Null were preoccupied with necromantic investigations and the philosophical contemplation of death.

The Drawing Down ceremony was held no more than once every lunar month, commencing on the night of the waning half-moon. Over a fortnight that culminated with the appearance of the new moon, dragons of every breed performed elaborate ceremonies of corporeal internment, consigning the spirits of the recently deceased to Null (in his aspect as Chronepsis).

During total solar eclipses, dragon followers of Null (in his aspect as Falazure) celebrated the Nullification. These zealots rampaged throughout Faerûn, leaving devastation and death in their wake. It was said that the one who inflicted the greatest number of mammalian casualties would be transformed into a Wyrm of Death, reigning as the high priest of Falazure until the next eclipse.

As for Null's non-dragon worshipers, their rites varied depending on the aspect they revered. A minor daily ritual for followers of Chronepsis involved turning over a personal hourglass to remind them of their own time trickling away. Often, a follower would turn over their hourglass before going to sleep, spending a moment or two contemplating the “empty” glass upon waking. In contrast, the rites of Falazure focused on the undead, involving either raising undead minions or working toward transforming oneself into a powerful undead creature.
