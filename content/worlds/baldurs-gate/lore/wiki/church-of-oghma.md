# Church of Oghma

The **Church of Oghma** was the primary religious organization devoted to the worship and service of Oghma, the Lord of Knowledge. Once a fully organized religion under a Grand Patriarch, the church splintered into individual philosophies following the Time of Troubles.

## Dogma
The Church of Oghma held that knowledge was supreme, especially in its purest form—the idea. Though formless, a simple idea could have the greatest influence on the world. In essence, knowledge was power, but it must be used responsibly and hiding knowledge from the world could never be a good thing. In turn, ignorance was a shackle of the mind, preventing beings from providing their full benefits to their fellows.

Hence, the faithful were instructed to encourage innovation and new ideas, no matter what they led to; gather and preserve knowledge so it was not lost; and disseminate knowledge as far as they could so it could be of benefit to everyone. They should also not suppress any new ideas, even if they seemed irrational or false, but instead allow them to be heard and considered freely. Yet they were also required to oppose people who promoted trickery, deception, and ignorance.

Commandments given to novices included:

- Spread knowledge whenever it is prudent to do so. Keep no secrets for their own sake.
- Curb and deny falsehood, rumor, and deceitful accounts and histories whenever you encounter them.
- Write or copy some lore of value and give it away freely at least once a year.
- Hide some writings away while distributing others widely so that the written knowledge of Faerûn is larger when you leave life than when you entered it.
- Sponsor, assist, and teach minstrels, bards, scribes, and recordkeepers whenever you encounter them and perceive a need.Spread truth and knowledge throughout the Realms so that all folk may know more.
- Never deliver a message falsely or incompletely, but always just as you receive it.
- Teach any folk who ask how to read and write or as much of these crafts as time and tasks permit—and charge no fee for this teaching.

Singers and bards were to be appreciated and listened to when first heard. A follower of Oghma should never kill a singer and should not stand idly by while a singer or bard was attacked.

### Disagreements
After separating, the Orthodox Church of Oghma in Procampur and the Oghmanyte Church in Sembia, later the Church of Oghma in Exile, disagreed on several points of doctrine. In the mid-to-late 1300s DR, the Sembian church was deeply conservative and emphasized that all knowledge must be applied and tested to determine that it had merit before it would be disseminated, rather than being hoarded away.

With the chaos of the Time of Troubles, as well as the terror wrought by the Spellplague, the clergy of Oghma generally withdrew, becoming cloistered guardians of any tome or scroll they could find. They hid away many precious sources of knowledge in the most remote reaches, hoping to safeguard them from all but the most world-shaking disasters. This attitude would prevail for most of the next century, and many troves of intellect would be locked away and forbidden.

Throughout the 1470s, attitudes towards the access of knowledge began to diverge. Some of the younger clergy, who had spent their lives storing away tomes after the tumult of the 14 century, began to promote the idea that "ideas should be as free as dragons wheeling in the sky," and that all knowledge should be free to all seeking it.

Another sect believed that knowledge should be organized into correct philosophies and schools of thoughts, so that those who sought knowledge would not be led astray. These philosophies would guide the readers and educate them; this education was a sacred duty that could not be cast aside in favor of "quantity over quality."

In the 1480s, the Orthodoxy prioritized recording the past and researching history in order to "know all that can be known about what is and has been." In contrast, the Exiles focused on using past knowledge to make predictions and preparations for the future, even arguing that it was unholy not to, as they believed that "knowledge of the past is the key to the future." The two approaches might be described as passive versus active, but sages like Elminster considered this a "gross oversimplification."

To rectify these differences, Oghmanytes south and east of the Sea of Fallen Stars proposed creating a unifying philosophy that would guide seekers of knowledge to an approved curriculum. This curriculum could also be used to advise Oghmanytes in library curation. However, despite spending decades debating on what the curriculum should be, these Oghmanytes were no closer to reuniting the church than when they started.

The largest temples of Oghma, including the House of the Binder, the Font of Knowledge, the House of Many Tomes, and the Leaves of Learning refused to take a public stance on these philosophies, preferring instead to privately debate them.

In the 15 century DR, there arose a belief (or heresy, depending on the faction) that Grand Patriarch Kordamant's fate had been concealed by Oghma as a test of faith for his priests: to determine whether they would hold to the quest of discovering and recording knowledge, or if they would fall prey to power struggles and reinterpretations of scripture.

## Organization
The Church of Oghma was open to all, welcoming people of every race and every philosophy. While clerics could be of any moral alignment, they tended to be neutral, lawful neutral, or chaotic neutral. The only requirement was that would-be priests vowed to follow the faith of Oghma, showed knowledge of its teachings, and devoted themselves to collecting, preserving, and applying knowledge.

Children raised within the church underwent the Naming ceremony at the age of twelve, or the equivalent age across species. In the private ceremony, a priest would reveal to the child their true name, which was to be used in private prayer only.

In order to be confirmed as a priest, an acolyte had to display either steadfast service to the faith, genuine loyalty to Oghma, or inspiration that was both good and useful. This was to be recognized by no less than two priests, who would then invest the acolyte as a true priest and bestow an official priestly title, such as Loremaster.

Traditionally, there were two types of clergy in the Church of Oghma: the cloistered ones who stayed in the abbeys, temples, and monasteries to read, write, and research, and the wayfaring ones who traveled the world to acquire new knowledge to bring back to them. The cloistered clergy were often scholarly sorts more comfortable with books than people, while the wayfaring clergy were usually curious, adventurous, and liked to live well.

These wayfarers were often vexed by petty church rules and squabbles, and by the cloistered ones' fussiness and pedantry, and wanted to get away and out into the real world. The two kinds occasionally had disputes on ideological matters, but rarely more than mild disapproval—they needed one another too much.

### Leadership
The Church of Oghma had been highly organized and well-established across Faerûn—one of the few fully organized religions in the Realms—with a recognized orthodoxy and a complete network of temples that adhered to it. Its entire hierarchy was dedicated to the service of the Grand Patriarch of Oghma, a pontiff traditionally based at the Domes of Reason in Procampur. Considered the "Voice of Oghma" on Toril, the Grand Patriarch's word was something the faithful would swear by.

### Schism
In the mid–14 century DR, the Grand Patriarch of Oghma was Cullen Kordamant. However, during the Time of Troubles in , Patriarch Kordamant and most of his supporting clergy vanished without a trace.

With the Church of Oghma left without an ultimate leader, the church fractured into a wide range of factions in the late 1300s, strenuously disagreeing on hierarchy and theology. The two most significant factions were the Orthodox Church of Oghma, based in Procampur, and the Oghmanyte Church in Sembia, later the Oghmanyte Church in Exile.

The largest faction, the Orthodoxy, refused to name a replacement nor recognize any other claimant to the title until it was confirmed that Kordamant was deceased, or until Oghma decreed it. The Sembian church, meanwhile, declared a new Great Patriarch.

The various churches and orders supported one faction or another, though not necessarily in their entirety; for example, the Pursuers of Pure Knowledge in Mintar supported the Sembians' view on the hierarchy, but did not agree with their theology.

### Titles
Up until around , all priests of the Church of Oghma were commonly referred to as Loremasters, regardless of gender. Regardless of rank, all priests addressed each other as 'Brother' or 'Sister,' while lay worshipers and nonmembers would politely refer to priests as Lord Loremaster or Lady Loremaster.
